From Vision to Conditions
How Principal Ife Oyedele Built the Conditions for
Co-Planning at Ánimo Watts
Green Dot Ánimo Watts is deeply committed to students with disabilities having the same
college, career, and life options as their peers. IGNITE gave Principal Ife Oyedele and her team
a way to act on that commitment: by strengthening the consistency and quality of co-planning,
alongside a community of schools doing the same work.

Why Watts Joined IGNITE
At Watts, the team sees access to college readiness for students with disabilities as an equity
commitment, not an add-on. They want every student to leave with real choices about college,
leadership and life.
The campus was already using co-planning when it joined IGNITE. The question was how to
make those practices consistent and strong for every teacher, year after year. IGNITE offered
the structure, coaching and network of peers to help them sustain the work and keep improving
it.
The team set a clear goal: increase the number of students with IEPs graduating A–G ready by
25%.
When Ife arrived five years ago, co-planning already happened informally among a veteran staff
with strong relationships. With IGNITE's support, she studied what made the strongest pairs
successful, then named and codified those practices so they could spread to other pairs and be
sustained across grade levels and content areas.

From Vision to Enabling Conditions
Ife served as both the local sponsor and the site team lead. She used that role to turn the vision
into everyday conditions for co-planning.
● Protected time in the school day. Ife worked to give every co-teaching pair a common
planning period. Pairs with protected time met consistently. One pair that met after
school instead kept running into barriers. For the next school year, every pair shared the
same planning period.
● A clear launch and expectations. Ife launched the practice directly with co-planners
and set clear expectations from the start.
● Regular progress monitoring. Ife met with co-planners every two weeks through her
instructional leadership team and held site huddles to review data and solve problems
together.
● A sharp focus on routines. The team narrowed its focus to consistent use of a
co-planning agenda and monitored each pair's progress. They named this their most
impactful change.
● Network supports, used well. Watts paired school-level structures with Green Dot's
co-teaching summer boot camp and quarterly co-planning days.
Ife sees the biggest shift as parity between general and special education teachers: both now
see themselves as teachers of every student in the room.

Results
In 2025–26, course passage for students with disabilities in Watts co-planning classrooms rose
from 62% to 83%. On NWEA MAP ELA, the share of students with disabilities at or near grade
level rose from 28% to 49%.

Lessons Learned
● Start with the why. A clear vision for students with disabilities gave co-planning a
purpose beyond compliance.
● Protect time in the school day. Time outside the school day was the hardest to keep.
● Lead it, don't just approve it. A principal acting as site team lead kept the work visible
and supported.

● Narrow the focus. Concentrating on one routine, the agenda, improved consistency
across pairs.

Try It at Your School
● Write one sentence connecting co-planning to your school's vision for students with
disabilities, and share it with co-teachers.
● Check your master schedule: does every co-teaching pair have common planning time
in the school day?
● Set a regular check-in, such as every two weeks, to review how pairs are meeting and
what is getting in the way.
