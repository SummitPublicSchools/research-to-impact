Set Your Vision for Inclusive Co-Planning
Leverage this resource to audit your current state and set a vision for co-planning.
This activity supports you in defining a clear vision for inclusive co-planning at your school — where the practice is headed and what it will make possible for your students and educators.
Directions: Use Part 1 to help you diagnose, visualize, write, and strengthen the future state of the practice. In Part 2, synthesize your thinking into a clear, concise, and inspirational vision statement you can share with stakeholders.
Companion Resource: Co-Planning for Differentiated Instruction: Executive Summary
Part 1: Audit Your Current State
What is the biggest obstacle to collaboration at our school?
Base your answer on your observations and reflections.

What is a bright spot we can build on?
What's already working that we can do more of?

What is our leverage point — one shift that would unlock the most progress?
This may be your most fragile enabling condition, or something else.

Part 2: Visualize the Future State
Looking ahead to the end of your first full year of implementation, what do you want to be true about co-planning at your school?
Think about your leverage point above — what will be different once you've moved it?

What do you want co-planning to look like, sound like, and feel like for students and adults when it is working well?
Look like
routines, behaviors, structures

Sound like
language, questions, collaboration

Feel like
confidence, clarity, belonging

How will students in your priority population, particularly students with disabilities, be better served by co-planning a year from now than they are today?

Write your vision statement.
It should be clear, concise, and inspirational — written so it can be shared with staff, families, and other stakeholders.

Who will you share this vision with first?
Make a plan to communicate it to others and build momentum for the practice to launch.

OPTIONAL Sentence Stems: Writing a Vision Statement
* Option 1: By [end of school year], students with disabilities at [my school] will __________ because general and special educators __________.
* Option 2: By [end of school year], co-planning [describe it] because educators ___________. In turn, students will ______________.
Example: By June of 2027, co-planning is a consistent, campuswide practice that ensures all students have access to rigorous, inclusive instruction. General and special educators collaborate regularly to design lessons that meet diverse learner needs — and our students with IEPs experience the same rich, grade-level learning as their peers.
OPTIONAL: Strengthening the Vision — Support & Scaffolds
These optional questions can be used to refine, test, and strengthen your vision by considering what effective spread, depth of quality, sustainability, and ownership of co-planning look like at your school.
Directions: Pick the questions that feel most useful. Use the “Think about” ideas as starting points, then go back to your vision statement and revise it. Focus on where you’re headed, not where you are today. Planning next steps comes after vision setting.

REACH: When co-planning is working well, which classrooms and students does it serve?
Think about: How many teams or classrooms? Why these classrooms? Are students with the greatest needs included? What would be true by the end of your first full year?
QUALITY: What would you see in lessons and classrooms that shows co-planning is making a difference?
Think about: How lessons change when teachers plan together. What students with disabilities can now access. What tells you it’s more than a meeting on the calendar.
SUSTAINABILITY: What would keep co-planning going over time, even if staff or leaders change?
Think about: Structures, habits and routines in place to ensure it lasts. What could get in the way (no need to solve it now). What co-planning looks like in a normal week.
OWNERSHIP: Who leads co-planning at your school, and how do they keep improving it?
Think about: How teachers and leaders share the work. Which roles naturally hold it. How the practice grows without outside support.
