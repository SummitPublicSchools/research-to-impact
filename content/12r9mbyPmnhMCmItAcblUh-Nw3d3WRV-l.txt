Co-Planning Kickoff Meetings
Launching strong co-planning partnerships from day one
The big picture: A kickoff meeting is the first partnership meeting a co-planning team holds. In
one focused session, general and special educators and their coach build the relationship,
shared aim, and routines that let every co-planning meeting after it focus on instruction, not
logistics.

What It Is: A Launch Point for the Partnership
A co-planning kickoff brings together a general education teacher, a special education teacher,
and their coach before routine co-planning begins. The team uses this time to:
● Get to know each other as professionals and partners
● Align on shared goals for the students they serve together
● Set logistics, roles, and norms with the Team Planning Tool, and adapt their co-planning
agenda
● Calendar their first co-planning meetings and coach touchpoints
WHO: General educator, special educator, and instructional coach. School leaders join when
possible to signal priority and protect time.
WHEN: Before co-planning starts, ideally during summer preservice. Repeat each semester or
when a partnership changes.

Why It Matters: Strong Starts Make Strong Partnerships
● Partnerships need intention. Research
shows that pairing two teachers without
attention to their professional dynamic
leads to an underused partnership.
● Clear roles reduce friction. Unclear roles
create confusion, strain working
relationships, and can delay services and
core instruction for students.
● Upfront decisions protect instructional
time. Teams that set time, roles, and
norms early spend their meetings on
instruction, not logistics.
● Early data routines speed up response.
When teams agree on what data they will
use and how, they identify student needs
and adjust instruction sooner.

IN THE FIELD

Collegiate Academies
The challenge. At the end of SY21-22,
Collegiate's academics team saw that
some co-taught classes started using
student data quickly while others took
longer to get going.
The shift. Leaders invested time earlier
through semesterly Kickoff Meetings
starting in SY22-23. After learning that a
whole-school kickoff did not allow for
differentiated support, they moved to
meeting with teaching pairs by content.

What happens. The co-teaching pair and their coach review students' April Dunn (AD)
plans, which give students with IEPs alternate ways to show mastery of state-tested
standards under Louisiana law. Teams build investment, clarify policies and procedures,
and set expectations for using their shared student data to reteach and reassess.
The rollout. Coaches attended network PD, leaders previewed the kickoff with each pair, a
turnkey deck shared the rationale, and coaches carried the protocol into weekly check-ins.

THE RESULTS
100% of students mastered the LEAP test or showed mastery of AD standards as of
January 2023. Collegiate exceeded its aim of 85% mastery by 15%.
Takeaway: Meet with each pair, not the whole staff. A differentiated kickoff gives teams the
specific support they need to start using data from day one.

Make It Work for Your Context
Adaptation notes and recommendations for the agenda provided below:
● Returning teams: Start with Section 6
to reflect on last year, then revisit
logistics, roles, and norms. Shorten
connection time.
● Short on time: Split into two sessions:
partnership and aim (segments 1-3),
then routines (segments 4-8).
● Build on preservice team formation:
Protect at least three hours before the
year starts, mixing formal meetings with
informal time like lunch or coffee. The
kickoff can then focus on routines.
● Introduce partners early: Pair
teachers as early as possible, ideally in
content where the special educator
feels confident.

● Differentiate by team: Meet with each
pair or content team rather than the
whole staff, or complete during a
co-planning preservice training. Tailor
support to each partnership.
● Launching mid-year: Use the same
agenda. Spend more time on enabling
conditions and ground norms in current
student data.
● Non-co-teaching teams: For push-in
or consultation models, agree on how
the special educator's input reaches
daily instruction between meetings.
● Make it a cycle: Hold a kickoff each
semester or when partnerships change,
and revisit Section 6 quarterly with your
coach.
