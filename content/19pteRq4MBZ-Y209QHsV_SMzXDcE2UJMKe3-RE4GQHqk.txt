Reviewing and Using Data: A Protocol for Improvement Teams
Improvement Teams at the school or district levels can benefit from a structured protocol for data reflection. This tool offers prompts and questions to support reviewing and using data together as a team. Use it whenever your team looks at data, including monthly implementation and growth monitoring or quarterly-to-annual data reviews on goal progress.
The protocol is designed to move your team from "just the facts" to interpretations, questions and implications for your work moving forward. Your team may generate additional questions or prompts over time. If so, add them to this list.
Before You Begin
1. Review norms. Ground your team in the norms to be held while reviewing data. Consider Data Norms and Pitfalls, by the National Equity Project, for ways to take a strong equity stance with the data.
2. Prepare the data. Bring a summary of key results with a visual representation (e.g., course passage data: 74% of students with IEPs and 83% of students without IEPs are passing the class with a C or better this month; 19% of 9th and 10th graders at our school have 1 or more incomplete assignments in January).
3. Center your priority students. Keep your focal population in view as you move through each step.
The Protocol
Move through each step in order. Resist jumping to interpretations before the team has named what it sees.
1. Data Dive
Ground in the data being reviewed. Take a few minutes to look at the summary of key results and its visual on your own before discussing.
2. Generate Data Statements: "Just the Facts"
Dig in and make some data statements together. Be prepared to share a couple with the group.
* I observe that...
* Some patterns or trends that I notice are...
* I'm surprised to see...
3. Interpretations
Draw out interpretations using statements like:
* I believe the data suggests that... because...
* One reason that we see these data trends could be...
4. Questions
Be curious about what you’re seeing and noticing in the data.
* What are we wondering?
* What can be clarified?
* What do we want to probe further around?
5. Implications for our work moving forward
* What does the data confirm that we already suspected?
* What questions come up that we'd be interested in exploring more?
* What should we act upon in order to achieve our goals?
Going Deeper: Additional Questions for Your Team
Use these questions to push your thinking at any step, or when your team gets stuck.
AREA
QUESTIONS TO ASK
Measures
* Is this the "right measure"?
* Is the measure/metric providing the information needed? Is the measure/metric accurate?
* Is the measure able to provide us data/information over time?
Data Collection
* Has the data been checked for accuracy? Has it been "cleaned"?
* Did data collection happen as frequently as needed?
* Is the data collection process feasible and efficient?
Interpretation
* Are we making assumptions?
* What could be an alternative interpretation or understanding of the data? Are we confirming our biases?
* If we represented the data differently, such as changed the visual or computed a different analysis, would our interpretation or meaning of the data change?
* Can we get more specific with data? Can we disaggregate data?
* Do we need to collect additional data or information to help us understand the data or make decisions?
* Are we centering our priority students when making decisions using this data?
Social Processes
* Whose perspective is missing in making meaning of the data?
* Who else needs to see this data and support taking action?
* What supports are needed to help people use this data or make sense of this data?
