Setting the Stage

“85% to 90% of students with disabilities can achieve at grade-level with the right supports.”

National Center on Educational Outcomes

“Yet only 50% of general education teachers feel strongly that students with disabilities can achieve at grade level.”

National Center for Learning Disabilities / Understood

The Challenge
Students with disabilities do not receive high-quality, differentiated instruction partly due to siloed educator training, mindsets that hinder effective collaboration, and packed schedules that don’t prioritize co-planning time.

This issue is compounded by a lack of effective planning and support among teachers.

As a result, the most disadvantaged students are at risk of falling behind.

What IGNITE Educators Are Saying
IGNITE educators agreed co-planning is valuable for meeting the needs of students with disabilities and useful for improving learning for all students. They also observed improved outcomes for students in their classrooms.

Reported benefits included:
Improved alignment between general and special education instruction (94.7%)
Better use of data to adjust instruction (94.7%)
Stronger collaboration and shared ownership with partner (84.2%)
More effective removal of barriers to access for individual students (78.9%)

Co-Planning Vision

What is Co-Planning

What is Co-Planning?
General and special education teachers meet regularly to:
align on content
evaluate student needs and data
remove barriers
select instructional approaches that meet the needs of all students, particularly those with disabilities

What Co-Planning Is and Is Not
IS
IS NOT
A structured, recurring meeting between educators
Focused on planning shared instruction for shared students
Driven by data, student needs, and upcoming content
A space for joint decision-making (not one person leading)
Results in clear instructional plans + differentiation moves
Just “touching base” or checking in
A logistics or compliance meeting
One teacher planning, one listening
Subjective or anecdotal decision-making
Only about accommodations or one student group
A last-minute or unprepared conversation
Co-planning is intentional, ongoing, data-driven planning that improves instruction for all students.

Implementing Effective Co-Planning

Unpacking an Effective Co-Planning Meeting
Strong co-planning:
A clear, aligned agenda and shared understanding of the learning goal
Frequent reference to specific students and their needs
Use of data to drive decisions
Conversation stays anchored in instruction
Differentiation is planned intentionally, proactively
Educators build on each other’s thinking - true collaboration!
The team leaves with clarity on instructional moves and roles
Discussion results in concrete next steps for the classroom

Effective co-planning is visible when teams move from
“what are we teaching?” to “how will we ensure all students learn it?”

Early Look-Fors for Strong Co-Planning
Consistent, protected time
Co-planning happens regularly and is treated as sacred time

A shared structure for planning
Teams consistently anchor in learning goals, student data/needs, and instruction
Conversations align to planning agenda or protocol

Specific, actionable outcomes
Plans include clear instructional moves, not just ideas
Differentiation is named and intentional
Roles and responsibilities during instruction are explicit
