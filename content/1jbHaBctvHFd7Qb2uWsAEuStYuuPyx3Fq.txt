Snapshot
WHAT IS THE PRACTICE?
The use of biweekly mass text-message communications to families, in both Spanish and
English, to notify families about their students’ academic progress and priority activities.
Families can respond directly to a school staff member via text message if they have
questions or concerns about their student.

WHY IS IT IMPORTANT?
• Traditional methods of communication with families (e.g., progress reports, teacher
letters, flyers) do not always reach families or students reliably or in a timely manner.
• Some families may struggle to attend events or meetings at school, which have
historically been key ways that schools engage families and communicate essential
information with families.
• Researchers found that daily communication between teachers and families immediately
improved homework completion rates, on-task behavior, and class participation,
and participants reported stronger teacher-student relationships, broader parental
involvement, and increased student motivation.1
• Students whose families are more knowledgeable and involved in their education have
been consistently shown to have better academic outcomes.2

The Challenge
Engaging Families after COVID-19
As students began returning to in-person learning from the COVID-19 pandemic, two of STRIVE
Prep’s network leaders, Jen Bisha and Jose Loera, realized that there was a need to intentionally
re-engage families to support student achievement. Their data revealed schools were having less
frequent interactions with families than in prior school years, and they believed increasing family
engagement was a factor they could meaningfully improve to increase student course passage
rates. After careful reflection, they agreed that the solution should consider several key factors:
• Families were not logging into the network’s parent portal that showed them their students’
grades and assignments.
• Sixty percent of students with disabilities were failing at least one class.
• Families needed communication available in both English and Spanish.
• Families indicated that text messaging was their preferred method of communication.
Jen and Jose decided that text message communication was the ideal family engagement strategy.
They chose to use ThruText, an online text messaging system for sending mass communications.
This tool would allow the STRIVE Prep team to communicate student-specific information, such as
failing grades and missing assignments, and establish a routine cadence for messaging to families.
To ensure that all families had access to this critical information, the system would send messages
in English and Spanish biweekly. ThruText would also prompt families to check students’ grades on
the parent portal and encourage them to engage in back-and-forth communication with staff. They
hoped this strategy would get every family involved and aligned with the school so families could
talk to their students about their education. These conversations would allow families and students
to build helpful habits for communicating with each other about academic progress and what
students need.

The Practice
Starting Regular Text Messaging Conversations
about Grades
Prioritizing the Needs of Students with Disabilities
At the beginning of fall 2021, Jen and Jose decided to implement the text messaging strategy only for
students with disabilities who were failing at least one class. As they launched this strategy, they quickly
realized that even students who were passing all classes needed to build habits with their families
around using the parent portal and communicating their needs at school. The team quickly expanded
the text messaging practice to send messages to all families of students with disabilities. As a result,
STRIVE Prep saw substantial improvements, with 39% of
students with disabilities increasing the number of classes
they were passing by the end of the fall semester.
Jen and Jose learned a few important lessons from this initial
implementation of text messaging with families of students
with disabilities. For the first text message, the team sent
an introductory video explaining to families the information
they would receive over the semester, how they could act
on that information, and strategies to support their students.
Unfortunately, not enough families watched the video when
notified with just a text message. As an alternative strategy,
the STRIVE Prep team posted the introduction video on each
school’s Facebook page and followed up with a text message
asking families, “Will you take 10 minutes to read this update
on your student’s progress?” This strategy resulted in twice as
many engagements with the video.

How STRIVE Prep Implemented
the Practice
Over time, STRIVE Prep learned important lessons on how to best implement their text message
intervention. They created simple and targeted messages, a carefully planned calendar of texts, and a
routine for sending text messages every other week. Families were able to anticipate when they would
receive text messages and could easily respond to the school and their students about students’ grades.

Anatomy of an Effective Text Message
Simplicity is key.
Keep messages very simple and focused on a specific purpose.
Parts to include:
1. Introduction
2. Student-specific information (e.g., number of failing classes)
3. Upcoming important dates and events (e.g., holidays, final exams, or school-specific activities)
4. A question to engage families

Expanding Parental Engagement to Benefit All Students
As they expanded the text messaging strategy to all students in the STRIVE Prep network, Jen and
Jose created a semester-long plan of all text messages that would go out to families, taking into
account holidays and other important dates to reduce the burden of sending text messages biweekly.
Importantly, they also decided to hire three “family ambassadors,” family members of students at a
STRIVE Prep school who would send and monitor text messages on a part-time basis. These family
ambassadors would be integral to the text messaging process, particularly in communicating with
families.
Each school principal created a video introduction to ThruText to share with families. The first text
message families received included the introductory video and expectations for future text messages
every other Thursday. Parents could opt out of the text messaging service, but no parents chose to
do so.
Once this introduction to the text messaging service happened and the process was fully active, Jose
routinely updated student information in ThruText every Thursday using a spreadsheet of grade data
pulled from the student information system.

Family ambassadors could then send biweekly text messages to inform families about students’
progress. Families could reply to the messages when they had questions or needed additional
information, and family ambassadors would respond. Family ambassadors were also responsible for
compiling responses from text messages to share with school staff.
On the alternating weeks when messages were not sent, Jose looked at data from ThruText to see
how many text messages were read, how many links were visited, and how many families responded.
Students’ advisors, school leaders, and teachers had the opportunity to use what was learned from the
responses and to identify how they could implement the text messaging strategy more effectively in
the future.

TIPS FOR SELECTING A TEXT MESSAGING PLATFORM
As STRIVE Prep’s team considered various platforms they might adopt for this pilot, they needed to
balance budget considerations between platform cost and time requirements for processing data. In the
end, they chose ThruText because it was a less expensive platform than others they considered. Yet, this decision
required considerable data management on the part of the STRIVE Prep team members, especially Jose, because it
did not integrate with their student information systems.
When determining which platform is best for a family text messaging system, consider the following:
• Cost of the platform
• Integration of the platform with existing student information systems
• Skill level of team members to run reports and merge content between systems
• Capacity of team members to track engagement and response data
• Training requirements for staff members and families to navigate the system

Barriers to Solve
Even after the text messaging strategy was fully implemented, some barriers remained for the team to
solve. One challenge was the limited access family ambassadors had to students’ information beyond
ThruText. Jen and Jose made sure that family ambassadors had the information that needed to be input
into text messages, but without full access to the data systems, family ambassadors could not update
information from families into the student information system nor retrieve details they needed about
students to respond to follow up text messages from families. In the end, the limited access family
ambassadors had to student data meant most data responsibilities lay with Jose. This both limited the
impact the family ambassadors could have on family engagement with the text messaging system and
put time burdens on Jose to complete data input and address follow-up requests on specific students.
The team also ran into a logistical issue where they could only match one student per phone number,
making it impossible to send text messages for two or more siblings at a STRIVE Prep school without
applying special assignment rules. For example, in cases where there were two students in the
household and two phone numbers for parents or guardians, the team could assign one student to
each of the numbers; this did not work, however, if there were more than two students attending the
school from the same household. Where the number of students from a single household exceeded the

The Impact
Using Text Messaging as a Way to Improve Family
Engagement in Student Grades
Despite some of the challenges related to implementing STRIVE Prep’s text message strategy, families
and students benefited from this method of family engagement. When STRIVE Prep surveyed families
about how receiving text messages impacted them:
• 100% of families said they talked to their student as a result of a text message,
• 74% of families said they checked their student’s grades as a result of the text messaging, and
• 78% of families said they felt comfortable talking to their students about their grades.
These results demonstrate that text messaging
accomplished the desired effect of helping families
communicate with their students about their grades and
develop habits around checking students’ grades.
One family expressed how much easier texting was
than other forms of communication schools often used:
“Text messages are very helpful. It helped me a lot
because I don’t use email that much.” The text message
strategy had the added benefit of ensuring that families
received text messages in multiple languages so that
engagement was not limited based on English fluency.
STRIVE Prep also succeeded in accomplishing their
goal of reconnecting the schools with families through a
regular communication strategy. One family expressed
their gratitude: “Thank you for always being aware of our
children and helping them in their school progress.”
