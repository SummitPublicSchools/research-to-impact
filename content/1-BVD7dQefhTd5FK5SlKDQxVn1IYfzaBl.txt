Using Exit Tickets to Drive Mastery
This tool helps educators shift from checking off assignments to diagnosing and driving students
toward deeper levels of understanding and skill development.

Step 1. Completion Inventory
Goal: Surface barriers to completion before diving into mastery.
1. Inventory the exit tickets at the start of your planning meeting to find missing or
incomplete work. Organize your inventory using a table or list, like the one below.
2. Identify patterns and assign immediate “completion actions” so you can focus on
mastery in step 2. One such completion action might be to quickly write the names of
students missing the assignment on blank exit tickets for future completion.

Step 2. Mastery Rubric
Goal: Move beyond task completion and “right/wrong” to growth in understanding and skill.
1. Define criteria for success. Choose one or two criteria based on focal standards from
instruction or consider priority student groups.
2. For the selected criteria, decide what emerging or developing skills look like. What does
it mean to meet expectations? What demonstrates mastery? Make note of these
decisions to apply them to the exit tickets. This can be done with a formal rubric, like the
examples below, or informally, via a quick sort using descriptions on sticky-notes.
3. Score each student’s work against your criteria for success. If scoring a large number,
consider a sampling approach to identify trends and patterns for subgroups.

Step 3. Action & Decision Grid
Goal: Link data to targeted instructional moves.
1. Plan follow-up for the group of students with missing and incomplete exit tickets.
2. Plan follow-up based on mastery of content. Follow-up activities may be designed for the
whole group or tailored to individual students based on their performance on evaluation

How to Implement in Your Team Meetings
1. Pre-work (48 hrs ahead): Share lesson objectives, exit-ticket prompts, and this tool.
2. Inventory (5 min): Fill in the Completion Inventory.
3. Score & Discuss (15 min): Apply the Mastery Rubric to a sample or all student work.
4. Data Conversation (10 min): Note distributions across levels; consult the Action Grid.
5. Targeted Planning (10 min): Define next-cycle instructional moves and assign owners.
6. Commit (5 min): Record action steps in your shared agenda.
By combining completion checks with clear mastery measures, your team can diagnose barriers
and design instruction that drives every student toward real understanding.
This tool was developed by Marshall CoLab in collaboration with educators across IGNITE. It reflects adaptations
and innovations from the field and is part of the growing Research to Impact Practice toolkit, a set of tools
developed as IGNITE Network resources, to advance outcomes for historically underserved students.
