Elements of Strong Co-Planning
ELEMENTS OF A STRONG CO-PLANNING MEETING
PRE-WORK
30-60 minutes
Each teacher completes role-specific pre-work, including:
* Review student data, including IEP goals, progress monitoring, and last year's TCAP practice assessment results.
* Identify specific standards or question types where students need additional support.
* Prepare instructional materials, accommodations, and potential scaffolds.
CONNECT AND SET
5 minutes
* Review (and refresh, as necessary) co-planning norms.
* Reconnect about what has happened since the last co-planning session.
* What changes or progress for students resulted from our last co-planning meeting?
* What lesson adaptations or test-taking strategies were most impactful? Why?
* Set intentions for this meeting:
* What lesson(s) or TCAP-aligned skills will we work on?
* What do we need to complete by the end of our time together?
* Which students or subgroups will be the primary focus of planning?
ALIGN
10 minutes
* Discuss the lesson objective(s) and how they align to Tennessee state standards and TCAP assessment expectations.
* What does the objective mean in the context of TCAP testing?
* What prerequisite skills, vocabulary, or test-taking strategies will students need?
* Align on end-of-class assessments or TCAP practice items.
* What does mastery look like on this standard or question type?
* What common misconceptions or error patterns might students show?
* Review the most challenging portions of lesson activities or test prep materials.
* Where are we likely to see the greatest student struggles?
* How will we use checks for understanding to identify and address misconceptions in real time?
REFLECT
10 minutes
* Analyze recent student data (classwide, subgroups, and individual students).
* What trends are emerging in TCAP practice performance?
* Are there discrepancies between students with disabilities and their peers?
* What is the root cause of these trends?
* Set 1-2 week micro-goals for prioritized students or subgroups. Example goals:
* By the end of the week, Students A, B, C & D will score >80% on TCAP practice questions aligned to Standard X with the use of their accommodations.
* The class average on the TCAP practice assessment for Standard Y will increase by 10% with a gap of <10% between students with and without disabilities.
REMOVE
10 minutes
* Address knowledge, vocabulary, and skill gaps that could hinder success on TCAP questions.
* Provide explicit instruction on how to use testing accommodations (e.g., text-to-speech, extended time) and practice self-advocacy by teaching students how to request accommodations during testing.
* Identify and model test-taking strategies that remove barriers for students on online tests, such as:
* Showing work on scratch paper.
* Using scratch paper to double-check answers.
* Annotating and organizing thinking using scratch paper before submitting answers.
* Incorporate positive praise aligned to the school-wide test prep initiative theme (e.g., "March Madness") to reinforce effort, perseverance, and strategic test-taking behaviors.
SELECT
7 minutes
* Choose high-impact co-teaching models (parallel teaching, station teaching, or alternative teaching) aligned to the lesson objective and students' needs.
* Identify opportunities for the special educator to:
* Use direct service time to reinforce tested skills.
* Provide small group instruction during general education lessons.
* Identify opportunities for the general educator to strategically group students and provide scaffolds during whole-group instruction.
* Identify ownership of directly teaching accommodations or test taking skills.
COMMIT
3 minutes
* Clearly log next steps in a shared, centralized location
* Assign action items to each teacher.
* Set a timeline for follow-up and data review before the next co-planning meeting.

Protocol for a High Impact Test Prep Co-Planning Meeting
Purpose: The goal of co-planning is to ensure that collaborating teachers develop high-impact, data-driven adaptations to lessons that meet the needs of all students, with a focus on preparing students for the TCAP assessment. This process draws on the expertise of both general and special education teachers to promote equitable access to grade-level content, close learning gaps, and maximize student outcomes.
The table below outlines the agenda elements and guiding questions of a 45-minute co-planning meeting. The guidance is designed to be flexible—teachers and coaches can prioritize specific sections based on their immediate needs, building toward full implementation over time.
