Set Your Vision for Repeated Reading for Oral Reading Fluency
Leverage this resource to audit your current state and set a vision for repeated reading.
This activity supports you in defining a clear vision for repeated reading for oral reading fluency at your school — where the practice is headed and what it will make possible for your students and educators.
Directions: Use Part 1 to audit your current state and name your leverage point. In Part 2, visualize the future state and write a clear, concise, and inspirational vision statement you can share with stakeholders. Use the Optional section to workshop your vision before you finalize it.
Companion Resource: Repeated Reading: Building Oral Reading Fluency in Secondary Readers: Executive Summary
Part 1: Audit Your Current State
What is the biggest obstacle to making gains in student literacy at our school?
Base your answer on your observations and reflections.

What is a bright spot we can build on?
What's already working that we can do more of?

What is our leverage point — one shift that would unlock the most progress?
This may be your most fragile enabling condition, or something else.

Part 2: Visualize the Future State
Looking ahead to the end of your first full year of implementation, what do you want to be true about the repeated reading practice at your school?
Think about your leverage point above — what will be different once you've moved it?

What do you want repeated reading to look like, sound like, and feel like for students and adults when it is working well?
Look like
routines, behaviors, structures

Sound like
language, questions, collaboration

Feel like
confidence, clarity, belonging

How will students in your priority population, particularly students with disabilities, be better served by the repeated reading practice a year from now than they are today?

Write your vision statement.
It should be clear, concise, and inspirational — written so it can be shared with staff, families, and other stakeholders.

Who will you share this vision with first?
Make a plan to communicate it to others and build momentum for the practice to launch.

OPTIONAL Sentence Stems: Writing a Vision Statement
* Option 1: By [end of school year], students with disabilities at [my school] will __________ because educators __________.
* Option 2: By [end of school year], repeated reading [describe it] because educators ___________. In turn, students will ______________.
Example: By 2028, repeated reading is embedded in every classroom as a joyful and effective routine for building fluency and confidence. Teachers use shared practices and tools to ensure all students — especially those with disabilities — see themselves as capable, thriving readers.
OPTIONAL: Strengthening the Vision — Support & Scaffolds
These optional questions can be used to refine, test, and strengthen your vision by considering what effective spread, depth of quality, sustainability, and ownership of repeated reading look like at your school.
Directions: Pick the questions that feel most useful. Use the “Think about” ideas as starting points, then go back to your vision statement and revise it. Focus on where you’re headed, not where you are today. Planning next steps comes after vision setting.

REACH: When repeated reading is working well, which students and classrooms does it serve?
Think about: How many students? Why these students or classrooms? Are students with the greatest needs included? What would be true by the end of your first full year?
QUALITY: What would you see in student and educator data and mindsets that shows repeated reading is making a difference?
Think about: How will student literacy scores and grades change? What behaviors or mindsets might you observe from students and educators (e.g., increased confidence)?
SUSTAINABILITY: What would keep repeated reading going over time, even if staff or leaders change?
Think about: Structures, habits and routines in place to ensure it lasts. What could get in the way (no need to solve it now). What repeated reading looks like in a normal week.
OWNERSHIP: Who leads repeated reading at your school, and how do they keep improving it?
Think about: How teachers and leaders share the work. Which roles naturally hold it. How the practice grows without outside support.
