Scaling Repeated Reading to Whole-Class,
Whole-School Implementation
This tool offers structured guidance for schools transitioning repeated reading from a
small-group intervention to a scalable, universal Tier 1 strategy. It addresses the need for
clearer frameworks and resources to help educators and leaders embed oral reading fluency
into the fabric of school-wide literacy instruction. This approach is supported by the Repeated
Reading chapter of the toolkit.

Guidance for Scaling Repeated Reading
A. School-Wide Conditions
To successfully embed repeated reading across classrooms, schools must first establish shared
beliefs and infrastructure. This includes prioritizing fluency in the school’s literacy vision,
protecting instructional time for fluency practice (5–10 minutes per day once routines are
established), and ensuring that teachers view fluency not as a remedial task but as essential to
comprehension and expression. These conditions foster ownership among general education
teachers and create the foundation for inclusive implementation.

Build shared understanding of fluency’s role in academic success.
Prioritize 5–10 minutes per day for fluency practice in schedules.
Leverage data cycles and instructional coaching to refine routines.

B. Tiered Instructional Models
A tiered model ensures that repeated reading is flexible yet coherent across student needs.
Establishing this structure helps schools scale the practice without diluting its impact or targeting
only a small subset of learners.
TIER

CHARACTERISTICS

Tier 1:
Whole-class

● All students engage in classroom-based fluency routines
● Embedded routines, peer supports, text variety

Tier 2:
Small groups

● Students receive additional small-group instruction based
on progress monitoring data
● Data-informed grouping, scaffolded feedback

Tier 3:
Intensive small
groups or individual

● Individualized or intensive support
● Multiple readings, weekly progress monitoring

C. Sample Instructional Routines
Teachers need practical, classroom-friendly routines to bring repeated reading to life.
Embedding repeated reading in core content allows for sustained exposure, routine practice,
and student engagement, especially when texts are culturally relevant or tied to student
interests. These routines make fluency visible and manageable within the flow of classroom
instruction.
Example Routines:
● “Fluency Fridays” with student-selected texts and recorded readings
● Partner reading integrated into language arts or social studies
● Culturally relevant reading tied to student interest and identity

D. Leadership and Coaching Systems
Sustainable scaling depends on leadership systems that support implementation and reflection.
Teachers benefit from seeing fluency modeled and from receiving actionable feedback on
pacing, student grouping, and progress monitoring. Without these systems, repeated reading
may lose consistency or be applied unevenly.
Example Systems:
● Fluency walkthrough tools to monitor implementation fidelity
● Teacher-led PLCs to review fluency data and share practices
● Video-based reflection and micro-coaching for feedback loops
By creating the right conditions, instructional pathways, and leadership supports, schools can
scale repeated reading as a meaningful part of Tier 1 instruction. The goal is not to replace
intensive intervention but to ensure that every student benefits from structured fluency practice.
