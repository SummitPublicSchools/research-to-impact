TEAM SYSTEMS FOR COLLABORATION TOOLKIT

Purpose:
A necessary condition for any type of teamwork is the creation and maintenance of effective systems and norms for collaboration. Co-teaching teams can only leverage their collective power if their daily operations are efficient and purposeful. With strong, thoroughly formalized systems in place, teams can maximize the effort and energy they are able to spend doing meaningful work for students.

About This Document:
This toolkit contains detailed guidance and examples for establishing the systems and norms necessary to effectively work together as a team. Tools include individual reflections and team meeting agendas for establishing correspondence norms, task delegation, a weekly schedule, and productive ways to manage conflicts within the team. Teams should use this toolkit to create durable systems and norms at the beginning of the school year, and may return or refer to it throughout the year if adjustments to norms are necessary.

Establishing a Team Schedule (Example & Template)
Below is an example of a weekly co-teaching schedule. Refer to this sample when creating your team’s schedule, making sure to designate time for all necessary co-teaching collaboration approaches.

Collaborative Prep Time: Co-teachers collaborate daily to internalize and individualize a lesson plan or two.
Daily Data Huddles: Co-teachers briefly adjust upcoming lesson plans in response to daily assessment data.
Weekly Planning Time: All team members meet weekly to collaboratively plan out next week’s lessons.
Coaching Check-ins: Weekly check-ins to debrief after coaches’ in-class observations and set next steps.
Post Assessment Data Collaboration: Co-teachers adjust upcoming unit plans after a major assessment.

Establishing Norms for Managing Conflict (Example & Template)
Co-teachers reflect on how their culture and socialization have shaped their views of conflict resolution. With this understanding, teams can work to agree on conflict resolution strategies with which everyone feels comfortable.

Pre-Work: Reflecting on Your Experience with Conflict
Read Elena Aguilar’s “Reflecting on Conflict” Reflection Prompts from her book The Art of Coaching Teams: Building Resilient Communities that Transform Schools. Each co-teacher should respond to those prompts individually, reflecting on how their upbringing has shaped their approach to navigating conflict, before reflecting on norms with the team to share experiences and create norms & expectations for managing conflict.

Creating Norms & Expectations
First, have both co-teachers complete the pre-work above independently. Next, share all individual preferences as a team and respond to the Independent Reflection questions. Designate a note-taker to capture key areas of similarity and difference in the Team Reflection & Norms. Then, discuss collective preferences, paying special attention to achieving consensus around areas of difference. Agree on acceptable and unacceptable behavior norms that both co-teachers can commit to, then formally record and distribute the norms and expectations.

Norms and Expectations for Managing Conflict Example
Team Reflection & Norms
Acceptable behavior
* Bringing up small issues privately as they arise to avoid resentment buildup.
* Calm discussion of specific situations using examples and “I” statements.
* Making constructive suggestions for how to avoid future conflicts.
Unacceptable behavior
* Undermining or sabotaging each other in front of students.
* Raised voices, charged language, intentionally hurtful comments.
* Ignoring or rejecting all proposed solutions to any conflicts that arise.
When/how could conflict arise?
* Any time (classroom, common prep time, etc.).
* When we are tired, stressed, annoyed, overwhelmed, or have divergent ideas.
What can we do (both individually and as a team) to prevent conflict from arising?
* Address potential issues as they arise, rather than building up resentment.
* Communicate frequently and constructively (formally & informally).
* Get to know each other on a personal level, but avoid personalizing the issues.
* Give each other the benefit of the doubt instead of assuming malintent.
When/how will we meet to ensure we stay ahead of conflict?
* Individual/small conflicts should be addressed within 24 hours
* Follow 24/48 rule: 24 hour cooldown, address it within 48 hours, or let it go.
* Our team will meet bi-monthly to reflect on the larger group dynamic during Team Stepbacks. By regularly reflecting on our work together and voicing our concerns, we will be able to address disagreements before they become problematic.
* Include a mutual facilitator (like our coach) whenever necessary.
What are our norms for resolving conflict and tension if it arises?
* Use “I” statements to ground feedback in your own experience and perspective and acknowledge that you cannot see all perspectives; assume the best intentions in others.
* Use specific examples whenever possible, and explain your intent/rationale.
* Remain solutions-oriented and forward-looking: there have been challenges in the past, but today will be about surfacing those challenges/moving forward.
* Remain grounded in impact on students: we are both here for students, so our feedback should help us understand how our actions impact student success.
* Genuinely seek to understand, not get defensive. Everyone has missteps, but the point is to understand those missteps’ impact on the team and move forward.
