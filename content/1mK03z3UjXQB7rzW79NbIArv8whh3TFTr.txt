Protocol for Staff Supporting
Repeated Reading
This tool provides clear, accessible protocols for support staff—including paraeducators,
instructional aides, and teaching associates—to help implement repeated reading with fidelity.
The guidance focuses on predictable routines, simple materials, and strong collaboration with
certified educators or specialists. These practices align with the scaling goals outlined in the
Repeated Reading chapter of the toolkit.

Purpose of the Protocol
Support staff can play a critical role in ensuring that students—especially those receiving Tier 2
or Tier 3 supports—get the repeated exposure to text and feedback they need. This protocol
clarifies expectations, structures routines, and provides support staff with enough direction to
confidently deliver high-quality practice sessions. When paired with teacher collaboration and
coaching, the protocol allows schools to expand access to fluency-building without
overburdening lead educators.

Core Responsibilities of Support Staff
Prepare materials in advance: copies of fluency passages, a timer, a pencil, and student
data sheets.
Facilitate repeated reading sessions 3–5 times per week as directed by the teacher or
coach.
Record and communicate student progress to the teacher, coach, or interventionist.
Follow clear, consistent routines to ensure fidelity.

Repeated Reading Routine (5–10 minutes)
Each session should follow this predictable structure:

1. Introduction & Purpose
● Greet the student and explain what you’ll be working on together.
● Share the purpose: “We’re practicing reading so it becomes smoother, easier, and
more automatic—so you can focus more on meaning.”
● Preview the passage (especially if content-aligned) and clarify key vocabulary if
needed. Remind the student to read smoothly, with expression, and to do their
best—not to worry about mistakes.

2. Conduct the Cold Reads
● Student reads the passage aloud for 1 minute. As the student reads, follow along on
your copy and mark errors (e.g., misread, omitted, added, hesitated).
● At the 1-minute mark, stop the student and note the last word read.
● Help the student:
○ Count total words read
○ Subtract errors to calculate Correct Words Per Minute (CWPM)
○ Divide CWPM by total words for accuracy rate (optional)
● Student records cold-read data on a progress monitoring graph.

3. Student Practice
● The student reads the passage aloud multiple times (typically 3–5).
● During these practice rounds:
○ Provide immediate corrective feedback using sentence stems (e.g., “You
missed this word—read the sentence again.”)
○ Focus on fluency features: pacing, phrasing, and expression.
○ Encourage use of strategies like finger tracking, chunking phrases, or
self-correction.
● Reinforce growth with prompts like “Let’s try to smooth that out even more in the next
round.”

After final practice, conduct a timed 1-minute hot read.
Follow the same steps as the cold read to mark errors and calculate CWPM.
Compare hot read score to cold read.
Student graphs both scores side by side (cold and hot bars) to visually track growth.
Celebrate progress—even small gains—and reinforce the connection to
comprehension.

5. Coaching Prompts
● Use brief reflection and goal-setting to build student ownership:
○ “What felt easier this time?”
○ “Did you notice where you slowed down or got stuck?”
○ “How did your expression sound when you read aloud?”
○ “What strategy helped you the most today?”
○ “What’s one thing you want to improve next time?”
● Encourage students to take pride in their effort and reinforce that fluency grows with
practice.

Appendix: Sample Tools for Support Staff
Tool 1: Repeated Reading Cue Card
Laminate this cue card for reference during each session. It helps ensure consistent
implementation and provides prompts for each step.
1. Introduction
● Preview the passage and clarify key vocabulary.
● Explain: “We’re practicing to make reading smoother and easier.”
● Remind the student to focus on expression, not perfection.
2. Cold Read
● Student reads aloud for 1 minute.
● Track errors (misread, skipped, added, hesitated).
● Count total words read and subtract errors for CWPM.
● Student records their score on their graph.
3. Practice Reads
● Student rereads passage 3–5 times.
● Give immediate, supportive feedback (e.g., “Try that sentence again”).
● Emphasize pacing, phrasing, and expression.
● Encourage strategies like tracking or chunking.
● Use prompts like: “Let’s smooth that out this time.”
4. Hot Read & Graphing
● Do a final 1-minute read.
● Repeat error tracking and CWPM calculation.
● Student graphs cold vs. hot scores.
● Celebrate effort and progress.

Tool 3: Praise & Prompt Script
Use these sample phrases to encourage students during repeated reading sessions.

Praise Examples:
● “Nice job staying focused!”
● “I noticed you corrected that word right away!”
● “You read that sentence much more smoothly the second time.”

Prompts for Error Correction:
● (Wait 3 seconds) “That word is ‘different.’ Try reading the sentence again.”
● “Let’s try that word together: ‘important.’ Now your turn.”
● “Let’s look at the first letter—what sound does it make?”
