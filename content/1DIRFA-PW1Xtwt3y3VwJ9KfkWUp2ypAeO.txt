Protecting the Space
How Nashville Classical Removed a Barrier to Co-Planning
When co-planning teams at Nashville Classical kept losing their meeting space, the site team
lead worked with them to find a simple fix: a reserved space next to their classrooms, marked
with a sign. Small systems changes like this one helped protect the space and time teachers
needed to plan for every learner.

Nashville Classical co-planning team

The Challenge
Nashville Classical’s team was committed to co-planning, yet their meeting space is limited, and
many rooms are shared by multiple people throughout the day. Though the school built its
schedule and assigned spaces for co-planning, teams would arrive to plan and find the room
already in use for things like standardized testing or an IEP meeting.
The time was on the schedule, but in practice, it wasn't protected.

What They Did
Co-planning teams shared the barrier with their site leadership in their biweekly team huddles.
The site team lead worked with the co-planning teams to gather data on the barrier and solve it
in a way that fit their campus.

● Identified the barrier together. Teams surfaced the
problem through their regular improvement routines
rather than working around it.
● Created a reserved space close to classrooms. The
team set up a reserved co-planning space right next to
teachers' classrooms.
● Made the reservation visible. A sign marked the
space as reserved for co-planning, so others in the
building knew it was already in use.
The change was small. Its effect was to remove a recurring barrier that affected every
co-planning pair at the school.

A Culture of Improvement
IGNITE Nashville highlighted Nashville Classical as a bright spot for pushing depth of practice
through a culture and systems of improvement. The team embraced a mindset of never perfect,
but always growing, and looked for small systems changes to remove barriers to
implementation.
Other signs of that culture:
● Co-teachers informally checked in on data they were seeing in the moment during
co-taught lessons.
● The team filmed some co-planning sessions to observe one another's practice.

Results
With co-planning time now consistent and protected, students in co-planning classrooms,
including students with IEPs, made meaningful gains in course passage across SY24-25 and
SY 25-26.

Authentic Voices
"Dedicated co-planning time with a purpose was the most meaningful because it
pushed us to collaborate and share ownership of lesson plans more consistently.
It was evident that scholars were aware of the shift in a positive way too."
Classroom teacher, Nashville Classical

Lessons Learned
● Scheduled time isn't protected time until the whole building can see it. A visible
signal helps others respect the commitment.
● Small fixes can unlock big barriers. A reserved space and a sign removed a problem
affecting every co-planning pair.
● Let teams name the barrier. The solution worked because the site team lead and
teachers identified it together through their improvement routines.
● Design for your context. Shared, limited space shaped the fix. Your school's barrier
may look different.

Try It at Your School
● Ask co-planning teams: What gets in the way of meeting when and where we planned?
● Walk the building during scheduled co-planning time. Are teams able to meet as
planned?
● Choose one small, visible change to protect co-planning time or space, and check back
in two weeks.
