and buy-in to skill development and consistency. Browse the General Guidance section for
broad direction, or use the Common Barriers table below to match a specific barrier to a
strategy.

STRATEGIES AND RESOURCES

Establishing and maintaining
clear roles and
responsibilities between
co-planning team members

Walk through the design planning space in the Team
Planning Tool to complete this process.
Use the Roles and Responsibilities checklist to clarify
General Educator and Special Educator contributions.

Protecting co-planning time
in your school schedule

Work with your site team to identify at least one specific
45 minute block weekly (co-teachers) or biweekly
(collaborators) in the master schedule (revisit pg. 3.11
in chapter).
Leverage resources like the TIES Center’s 5-15-45 tool
to enable strong, abbreviated co-planning while building
up to more dedicated planning time.
Establish accountability routines, such as leadership
check-ins, protocol observations, and collaborative
reflections.

Using a co-planning agenda
with fidelity and consistency

Strong co-planning meetings that result in effective
collaborative teaching leverage four key components:
alignment, reflection, removal, and selection (revisit pg.
3.5, 3.24 in chapter).
Identify which agenda works best for the team by
exploring different Co-Planning Agenda Templates —
from basic to guided.

Selecting the most
appropriate co-planning
structures

Use the How to Leverage Different Co-Planning
Structures tool to understand each model’s purpose,
structure, and function to match your collaboration
approach to the intended impact.

Completing prerequisites for
co-planning meetings

Assign role-specific pre-work and reinforce
expectations about being prepared (revisit pg. 3.15 in
chapter).
Collect data prior to meetings.
Preparation of lessons/materials prior to planning.

Analyzing data to drive
instructional
decision-making

Data drives effective instructional planning. Revisit A
Collaborative Approach to Student Work Analysis,
specifically Green Dot’s Student Work Analysis Protocol
(pg. 4.22)
Focus on moving from work completion to content
mastery. Review the Using Exit Tickets to Drive
Mastery tool.
Establish and spiral roles and responsibilities.
