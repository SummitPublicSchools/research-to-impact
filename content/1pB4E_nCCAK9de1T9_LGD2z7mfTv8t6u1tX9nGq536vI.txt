Setting the Stage

“85% to 90% of students with disabilities can achieve at grade-level with the right supports.”

National Center on Educational Outcomes

“Yet only 50% of general education teachers feel strongly that students with disabilities can achieve at grade level.”

National Center for Learning Disabilities / Understood

The Challenge
Students with disabilities do not receive high-quality, differentiated instruction partly due to siloed educator training, mindsets that hinder effective collaboration, and packed schedules that don’t prioritize co-planning time.

This issue is compounded by a lack of effective planning and support among teachers.

As a result, the most disadvantaged students are at risk of falling behind.

Co-Planning = Effective Part of the Solution
Co-planning is a high-leverage response to the challenge.
Co-planning requires leadership. Leaders must actively champion co-planning as a driver of better teaching and improved instructional outcomes

Why Leaders Matter?
School leaders signal whether co-planning is a priority for improving outcomes or just another initiative
Leaders help turn a promising practice into a schoolwide way of working
Leaders shape whether collaboration stays focused on meeting the needs of students with disabilities
Leaders create urgency, coherence, and follow-through around the work

When leaders champion co-planning, schools are more likely to build shared ownership for all students

Why Leaders Must Champion Co-Planning
Co-planning is a key strategy for improving access, alignment, and outcomes for all students - including students with disabilities
It helps move schools from siloed support to shared responsibility for all learners
School leaders determine whether this work is prioritized, reinforced, and sustained
Without leadership, co-planning is unlikely to become consistent enough to improve outcomes
With leadership, co-planning can become a driver of stronger instruction for students and teachers alike

What is Co-Planning?

What is Co-Planning?
General and special education teachers meet regularly to:
align on content
evaluate student needs and data
remove barriers
select instructional approaches that meet the needs of all students, particularly those with disabilities

Why is Co-Planning Important?
Shared understanding of needs of all students
Clear roles and responsibilities for instructional delivery
Differentiated instructional approaches
More accessible and supportive learning environments

This map shows that co-planning is a cycle, not a standalone event. Leaders set the conditions up front — schedule, expectations, agenda, data, and support — and then teams run repeated cycles of planning, teaching, collecting data, and reflecting. Our role is to make sure that cycle is protected, supported, and strong enough to actually improve instruction for students.

Set the Conditions for Co-Planning
Co-planning is only possible when the right mindsets, infrastructure, and resources are in place.

Communicate that all educators are responsible for all students
Set a clear expectation for lesson-focused co-planning
Define roles and responsibilities for teacher partners
Build a schoolwide schedule with dedicated co-planning time
Ensure teams have access to high-quality instructional materials
Ensure teams bring and use student data, learning profiles, and accommodations
1
What leaders must put in place
Shared vision and mindset
Protected time and clear structures
Materials and data for planning
!

Sustain the Practice of Co-Planning
Co-planning becomes sustainable when leaders protect time, reinforce expectations, and build routines that support collaboration.

Protect co-planning time as a schoolwide priority
Train staff on the purpose and non-negotiables of collaboration
Hold all educators accountable for engaging in co-planning
Launch the year with team formation and norm-setting
Build routines for note-taking, agenda use, and meeting follow-up
Remove obstacles by limiting unnecessary tasks and aligning planning expectations
2
Critical things to attend to
Is co-planning time actually preserved?
Do staff experience this work as shared ownership?
?

Build Staff Skill - Improve Quality Over Time
Strong co-planning requires ongoing staff development, coaching, and support - not just a schedule and clear protocol.

Launch the work with a partnership or school-year kickoff
Provide ongoing capacity building on collaboration
Offer coaching on data analysis, planning, and lesson adaptation
Observe or facilitate co-planning meetings regularly
Differentiate support based on team experience and need
Use student work, exit tickets, and planning artifacts to guide coaching
3
Guiding questions for leaders
Do teams know how to collaborate effectively?
Can teachers analyze data and plan from it?
Are we building confidence and capacity over time?

The Leader’s Role

A Quick Refresh: The Leader’s Role
Leaders do not run every co-planning meeting, but they shape whether high-quality co-planning can happen consistently and well.
Leaders are responsible for:
setting the conditions
sustaining the practice
building staff skill and improving quality over time

From Leadership Role to Leadership Action
Knowing the leader’s role is the starting point. The next step is understanding what leaders must actually put in place to make strong co-planning possible.

To move co-planning from idea to practice, leaders need to attend to a few core implementation levers:
time and structure
vision, roles, and collaboration culture
agendas, pre-work, and data use
coaching and capacity building
monitoring implementation quality
As we go deeper, consider:
Which of these levers feels strongest at your site right now?
Which one most needs leadership attention?
?

Protecting Time and Structure
Even strong teams cannot co-plan well without protected time and a clear structure.

Build dedicated co-planning time into the schedule
Protect that time from being repurposed
Set expectations that co-planning is for instructional planning
Ensure teams use a shared meeting structure or agenda
Create accountability for how planning time is used
1

Building Vision, Roles, and a Culture of Collaboration
Co-planning is stronger when leaders create shared ownership, role clarity, and a culture where collaboration is expected.

Communicate that all educators are responsible for all students
Launch teams with norm-setting and team formation
Define roles and responsibilities for teacher partners
Reinforce shared ownership through messaging and routines
Hold staff accountable for engaging in meaningful collaboration
2

Designing Agendas, Pre-Work, and Data Use
A co-planning meeting is only as strong as the structure and evidence that guide it.

Adopt a common agenda or co-planning protocol
Clarify expectations for role-specific pre-work
Ensure teachers bring lesson plans, materials, and student data
Make student work and learner needs central to the meeting
Support teams in planning for mastery, not just work completion
Reinforce use of a common protocol for student work and data analysis
3

The Co-Planning Meeting Agenda
ALIGN on content
REFLECT on student data
REMOVE barriers to access
PLAN for future, differentiated instruction
Facilitation: Spend time here - unpack, annotate, print out

Coaching and Capacity Building
Strong co-planning requires more than time in the schedule — it requires support, feedback, and skill-building.

Provide ongoing coaching on collaboration and planning
Observe or facilitate co-planning meetings regularly
Differentiate support based on team experience and need
Use student work and exit tickets in coaching cycles
Support general educators with planning moves and special educators with content-aligned collaboration
Revisit roles, expectations, and meeting quality over time
4

Support for co-planning
observe at least 1x monthly to ensure high-quality implementation
followed by observation of co-planned lesson to identify further development needs

Lesson planning support for general educators
helps ensure that co-planning is focused on student-specific adaptations

Content support for special educators
bolster content knowledge, provide additional curriculum training
When Needed: Coaching for Co-Planning

Monitor Implementation Quality
Leaders should monitor not only whether teams are meeting, but whether teams are co-planning well.

Leader look-fors:
Teams are meeting consistently
A shared agenda or protocol is being used
Student data is present and discussed
Teams are identifying barriers to access and designing differentiation moves
Instructional roles are clarified
Meetings result in clear next steps and actionable plans
