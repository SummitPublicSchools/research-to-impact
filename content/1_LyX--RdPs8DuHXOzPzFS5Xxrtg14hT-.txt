Tips for Supporting Peer-to-Peer Feedback
in Repeated Reading
Repeated reading with a peer partner allows students to practice reading in a socially supportive
context. This approach gives students frequent, supported practice with reading fluency while
building accountability, collaboration, and confidence. Partner reading can serve more students
simultaneously, as one teacher can monitor multiple partners at the same time. This effective
model of repeated reading is most successful
when students (and educators) are trained and
supported in giving and receiving feedback
effectively.

Tip #1: Implement training and support for staff
Staff must develop the knowledge and skills around the practice, understanding reading fluency
and learning how to prompt students to give feedback to each other.

Model the practice in professional learning spaces
● During professional development, simulate the repeated reading routine so educators
can experience the timing, flow, and coaching prompts firsthand.
● Debrief how each component supports fluency and the overall effectiveness and
implementation of the practice.

Establish a clear feedback routine
● Train educators in a consistent approach to modeling and reinforcing effective
feedback.
● Provide sample language, sentence stems, and visuals for the classroom.

Create a feedback framework or CFU (Check for Understanding)
● Develop a shared set of indicators that define high-quality peer feedback.
● Use the framework during observations and coaching conversations.

Provide ongoing coaching for staff
● Offer opportunities for teachers to practice the feedback routine, receive coaching on
facilitation moves, and reflect on student engagement and progress

Tip #2: Build students’ skills and mindsets to give and receive
feedback.
Students need time and practice to develop comfort and confidence around feedback: ongoing
practice and coaching around giving feedback, as well as normalizing/rationalizing the benefits
of receiving feedback.

Module feedback in action
● Demonstrate how to give and receive feedback respectfully, modeling both celebration
(“I liked how you used expression there”) and correction (“You skipped this word - try
that line again”)

Connect feedback to purpose
● Explain why feedback matters - good readers notice and correct their errors to improve
accuracy, fluency, and comprehension
● Continuously reinforce what fluent readers do

Use teaching and visual supports
● Use slides, anchor charts, posters, or cue cards with prompts and examples of how to
give feedback (see school spotlight below)

Provide sentence stems
● Offer structured language such as: “I noticed you...” “Try reading this word again...”
“That sounded really smooth...”

Model teacher vulnerability
● Have educators read aloud to students, intentionally making errors, and have students
give them feedback. This normalizes making mistakes and models receptive
responses.

Practice before implementing
● Give students time to role-play giving feedback to a partner and debrief what felt helpful
and respectful.

Tip #3: Strengthen and scale adult and peer coaching.
Students benefit from real-time prompting and support to build their skill and improve overall
implementation. Improving the quality and quantity of coaches enables the practice to expand
across more classrooms and sites.

Train educators
● Train educators and staff supporting the practice in coaching the peer coach on their
feedback

Provide real-time coaching support to students
● Don’t step in and do it for students — support them in their skill development
○ Start with a celebration (name an observed positive coaching move)
○ Share 1-2 things they could improve on (the errors)
○ Provide them with coaching prompts (see above) to try on
● Create a cycle of educator training in which staff observe one another’s coaching of
students to increase the number of quality coaches who support students during the
practice
