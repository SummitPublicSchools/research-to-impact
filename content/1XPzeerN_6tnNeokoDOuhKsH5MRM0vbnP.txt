Implementing Repeated Reading Across
Multiple Tiers of Support
A Decision-Making Checklist for Practitioners and Coaches
This tool supports educators in using student data to make informed decisions about how to
implement repeated reading effectively across multiple tiers of support. Grounded in
Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS) and Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles,
this checklist ensures that repeated reading is tailored to meet students’ diverse fluency needs
while remaining scalable. This approach aligns with the updates recommended in the Repeated
Reading chapter.

Why These Elements Matter
Data Review &
Entry Criteria

Repeated reading is most effective when it targets students who have
already acquired basic decoding but struggle with automaticity and
expression. Entry decisions should be based on triangulated data (e.g.,
DIBELS, WCPM, IEP fluency goals), ensuring the right match between
student need and intervention strategy.

Group Size

Smaller groups allow for more targeted, corrective feedback and
scaffolded modeling. However, whole-class implementation is key for
inclusive Tier 1 fluency development and reduces the stigma often
attached to "intervention groups."

Frequency

Research shows fluency gains correlate directly with the number of
repeated exposures to text per week. More frequent sessions benefit
students with greater need, but even low-frequency routines build
consistency and classroom reading culture.

Intensity

Intensity is shaped by the complexity of the support—timing, scaffolding,
error correction, and teacher involvement. Intensity doesn't mean
harder—it means more precise and more tailored.

Exit Criteria

Clear, pre-determined exit points avoid "intervention drift" and ensure that
support is removed once a student shows sustainable growth. Exit criteria
also affirm student progress and promote self-efficacy.

learners
● Peer learning labs or coaching cycles focused on oral fluency strategies

By equipping educators with both the checklist and the reasoning behind it, this tool supports
not only technical decision-making but also builds professional judgment and instructional
coherence across settings. Coaching routines ensure that implementation is not only consistent
but continually improving. Full guidance can be found in the Repeated Reading chapter.

This tool was developed by Marshall CoLab and reflects adaptations and innovations from the field. It is
part of the growing Research to Impact Practice toolkit, a set of tools developed as IGNITE Network
resources, to advance outcomes for historically underserved students.
