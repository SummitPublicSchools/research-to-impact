Common Barriers and Pitfalls
in Routine Data Cycles
Every team hits barriers when implementing routine data cycles. Those barriers shift as
implementation deepens, from mindsets and buy-in to skill development and consistency.
Browse the General Guidance section for broad direction, or use the Common Barriers table
below to match a specific barrier to a strategy.

Common Barriers
MOST COMMON BARRIERS

Limited time to collect or
analyze student data

PREVENTION AND MITIGATION
STRATEGIES AND RESOURCES
Build 10–15 minutes of data review time into existing
weekly team meetings or PLCs.
Use streamlined dashboards that auto-populate from
SIS or require minimal manual entry.
Design usable teacher-facing data dashboards and
provide ongoing training to support implementation.
● Check out How-to Design and Prototype a Data
Dashboard.
Designate a data lead to prepare data ahead of
meetings.
Pair data review with planning routines—avoid
separating them into different meetings.
Provide quick data-check protocols, so teachers focus
on patterns, not perfection.

Difficulty planning / designing
effective interventions based
on data

Use a structured data-to-action protocol. This includes
steps to analyze trends, name focus areas, set
SMART goals, and choose interventions (review pgs.
3.17–3.18, 3.28–3.29 in chapter).
Embed coaching in the data cycle. Coaches support
teachers in interpreting the data and designing precise
instructional responses (review pg. 3.21 in chapter).
Review student work alongside the data. This helps
clarify misunderstandings and informs stronger
intervention choices.
Create and use an intervention strategy bank.
Ready-to-use strategies aligned to common needs
reduce planning time and improve the quality of
interventions.

Limited time to deliver
interventions
(Schedule challenges or
student absences interrupt the
delivery of intervention)

Identify flexible delivery opportunities. Examples
include small-group time, workshop rotations, or
embedding supports into upcoming lessons.
Right-size interventions to available time.
Interventions can be delivered through reteaching
within core lessons, spiraled review, pre-teaching, or
focused small groups—not just through pull-outs or a
separate block.
Use collaborative planning structures to help design
interventions that are feasible within the available
schedule (review pg. 3.21 in chapter).
Build a culture that prioritizes intervention time.
Protect intervention delivery in the same way schools
protect co-planning or collaborative time.
