Resetting the System
How STEM Prep Academy Used Coaching
to Remove Barriers to Collaboration
In January 2026, STEM Prep paused, retooled its co-planning agenda for four-person teams,
and put coaches in every grade-level meeting. The big lesson: when more than two educators
plan together, teams need direct coaching to learn how to collaborate well, and leaders need to
keep adapting the routine to fit their students and teachers.

The Challenge
STEM Prep delivers a rigorous, inquiry-based education with a strategic focus on science,
technology, engineering, and mathematics. Its co-planning work sits inside a campus culture of
data-mindedness. IGNITE amplified a commitment to data-driven instruction that was already in
place. Subgroup data is a key lens for STEM Prep educators, especially for English learners
and students with exceptionalities.
Co-planning is embedded in the school's vision. STEM Prep adapted the practice so four
educators plan together for a shared cohort of students.
The model was clear, but meetings were unproductive. Teams weren't using protected time well,
worked together inconsistently, and struggled to plan across multiple courses.

Managing a four-person agenda was hard. Teams got stuck in the "Align" phase and rarely
completed the full planning cycle. The site leadership team saw a need for more modeling and
coaching.
The goal was for teams to get through an entire co-planning meeting more effectively and
efficiently, completing every step together.

What They Did
Coaches set out to revise the co-planning process so every educator could participate and
every team could finish the full cycle. Implementation was scaffolded so teams would eventually
lead their own sessions, leaving each one with a goal for applying their planning to student
learning.
Before: four-person teams met but stalled early, with uneven participation and no consistent
routine.
What changed in January 2026:
● Updated materials. Leaders revised the agenda and resources to match STEM Prep's
four-person model, then trained teams in it.
● A dedicated coaching team. Four school leaders formed the IGNITE co-planning
coaching team: assistant principals, instructional coaches, and special education
leaders. Each coach owned one grade level and joined every co-planning meeting for
that grade, weekly, throughout January.
● Three coaching focus areas: preparation for meetings, supportive accountability, and
equal representation of every educator's voice.
● Gradual release. Coaches first facilitated, then coached, then stepped back as teams
gained skill with the agenda and resources.
● One coaching team, not four coaches. Coaches set shared norms and debriefed
regularly on what was and wasn't working, so each grade had a consistent experience.
After: teams use the revised agenda and resources to build collective skill to serve their full set
of learners.

Lessons Learned
● Bigger teams need direct coaching. Collaborating effectively and efficiently doesn't
happen on its own, especially when the model goes beyond two educators.
● Adapt the routine to your goals. The leadership team had to decide what it wanted
co-planning to accomplish, then modify the routine to meet the needs of students and
the educators serving them.
● Coaches learned by living the agenda first. Experiencing the revised agenda
themselves, then coaching teams through it, showed coaches how it needed to work in
practice.
● Remove barriers in real time. Being in the meetings let coaches spot problems as they
happened and solve them together, adjusting the adaptation to fit their campus.
● Data mindsets and rigor still matter. Teams needed support using data and moving
through the final steps that bridge co-planning and co-teaching.
