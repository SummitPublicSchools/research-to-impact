From New to Confident
How Marion P. Thomas Teachers Learned to Teach and
Use Data at the Same Time
New special education teachers at Marion P. Thomas Charter School learned to teach and to
use data at the same time. With a biweekly team huddle, coaching, and protected time, they
built a routine they came to own. By the end of the year, they were running data cycles more
often than asked, on their own, and every teacher said the work strengthened their commitment
to staying in education.

Marion P. Thomas RDC teachers meet with their coaches in a coaching huddle.

The Challenge
Many of Marion P. Thomas's special education teachers were brand new to teaching, and new
to the special education role. They needed to learn how to teach while also learning how to use
data to design and inform their instruction.

Routine data cycles asked for a more rigorous skill than many of these teachers were ready for.
The school also had a history of being flagged for improvement, so data often felt like an
accountability measure rather than a tool for learning. IGNITE asked teachers to see data
differently: as a quick, low-stakes way to learn and respond to what students need.
The team needed a way to support new teachers to grow as educators while using data in a
timely way, so no one waited for students to fail or left students behind.

What They Did
IGNITE Newark leaders worked with site leadership to fill gaps in site readiness, adding training
and leadership resources. Together, they built a model that paired teacher skill-building with
implementation of the routine data cycles practice.
● A team routine. Teachers formed an IGNITE cohort and ran routine data cycles together
in a huddle at least every other week, with support from each other and their coach.
● Protected time. Leaders doubled down on protected time for data review and response,
so the routine did not compete with everything else on a new teacher's plate.
● Coaching alongside teaching. Coaching helped teachers build core teaching skills and
data skills at the same time.
● A new mindset about data. The team framed data as information for learning and
planning, not judgment.
As teachers grew more familiar and confident collecting and analyzing data, they started
running cycles on their own, beyond the biweekly huddle.

Results
Teachers built quality and independence over the year.
Cycle quality rose steadily across the year and finished the highest in the IGNITE network.
Teachers ran cycles more often than the every-other-week huddle required, and most cycles
(81%) were run independently by the end of the year.

Teachers saw the routine change their practice.
All five teachers reported reflecting on student progress more often. Four reported more
intentional, targeted planning, and three reported stronger data analysis.

IGNITE built a community teachers wanted to stay in.
Five of five participating teachers said IGNITE strengthened their commitment to staying in
education. All five planned to continue routine data cycles.

Lessons Learned
● Meet new teachers where they are. Pair data skill building with core teaching support,
so the routine builds confidence rather than adding pressure.
● Start together, then let teachers fly. A shared huddle cadence gives new teachers a
safe place to practice. Independence follows confidence.
● Reframe data as learning. In schools where data has felt like accountability, naming
data as a tool for teachers changes how they engage.
● Community keeps people. A team that learns together gives teachers a reason to stay.
