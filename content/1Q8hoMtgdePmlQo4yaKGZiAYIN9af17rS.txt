Executive Summary
Co-Planning for Differentiated Instruction
Co-planning is a high-leverage practice that enables general and special educators to
collaboratively design instruction that meets the unique and specific needs of all students they
teach. This executive summary distills key guidance from the Co-Planning for Differentiated
Instruction chapter into a streamlined reference for school and system leaders on the why, what,
and how of co-planning.

Why Co-Planning is Important
Quality co-planning provides both rigor and
accessibility, strengthens instructional
alignment, and expands teacher capacity to
address barriers to learning. It ensures
equitable access to quality instruction for all
students, regardless of their learning profile or
disability status.

Effective Co-Planning Defined
Collaborative planning happens in many forms, including regular planning meetings and deeper
unit planning or data analysis meetings, and informal check-ins. When implemented effectively,
the practice is deeply embedded, sustainable, and situated within an ecosystem of collaborative
structures and routines that are:
● Scheduled and Protected: Happens on a consistent, recurring basis: ideally weekly (for
co-teachers) or biweekly (for collaboration teams); is shielded from other obligations
● Role-Aligned: Builds from a clear and evolving understanding of who brings what to the
planning process, guided by a shared Roles & Responsibilities checklist
● Data-Driven and Student-Centered: Anchors analysis in student work, learner profiles,
and formative data to drive decisions and adapt instruction

● Structured by an Agenda: Follows a protocol with four key elements: Align, Reflect,
Remove, Plan
● Model-Aligned: Plans instruction tailored to student needs using one of three models:
full-week plan, exemplar lesson plan, or focal group plan
● Coached and Supported: Provides regular feedback, reflection, and targeted coaching
to strengthen collaboration and instructional planning
