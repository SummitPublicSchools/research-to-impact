Scripts for Discussing Fluency Interventions
Phone Script for Notifying Families About the ORF Intervention
Introduction:
"Hello, this is [Your Name] from [School Name]. I’m calling to share an exciting opportunity for
[Student’s Name] to receive additional reading support through a fluency intervention program
we are offering. Do you have a quick moment to chat?"

Why We’re Calling:
"We’ve identified that fluency—reading smoothly, quickly, and with expression—is an area
where [Student’s Name] could benefit from extra support. Based on recent assessments, we are
inviting them to participate in a six-week reading fluency program."

How the Program Works:
● "[Student’s Name] will work in a one-on-one or small group setting for three short
sessions a week—just 15 to 20 minutes each."
● "They’ll practice reading engaging passages at their level, get individualized feedback,
and track their progress over time."
● "Our goal is to help them build confidence, improve fluency, and strengthen their overall
reading skills."

How You Can Help at Home:
"A great way to support this at home is by encouraging [Student’s Name] to read aloud for 5-10
minutes daily. Asking them to read expressively and helping with tricky words can make a big
difference! Listening to audiobooks while following along in the text is also helpful."

Closing:
"We’re excited about this opportunity and believe it will have a positive impact on [Student’s
Name]’s reading growth. If you have any questions, feel free to reach out to me at [Your Contact
Info]. Would you be open to this support for [Student’s Name]?"
(Pause for response. Answer any questions they may have. If they agree, thank them and
confirm next steps.)
"Thank you for your time! We look forward to seeing [Student’s Name] grow as a reader."

Parent Letter for Notifying Families About the ORF Intervention
Dear [Parent/Guardian’s Name],
We are excited to share that [Student’s Name] has been selected to participate in a reading
fluency intervention designed to help students become more confident, fluent readers. This
six-week program will take place in a one-on-one or small group setting for three short sessions
per week (15-20 minutes per session).

Why was your child chosen?
Fluency is a critical skill in reading—it allows students to read smoothly, quickly, and with
expression, which helps them better understand what they read. Based on recent assessments, we
identified that [Student’s Name] would benefit from additional support to improve reading fluency.

How does the program work?
● Weekly Practice: Students will read engaging passages at their level, receive guided
practice, and track their progress..
● Celebrating Growth: Each student works toward a personal fluency goal, and we celebrate
their improvement along the way!

How can you support your child at home?
Encourage your child to read aloud daily for 5-10 minutes. Ask them to read expressively and help
them with tricky words if needed. Listening to audiobooks while following along in a text can also
be helpful!
We are excited about this opportunity and believe it will have a positive impact on your child’s
reading confidence and success. If you have any questions, please feel free to reach out to
[Teacher’s Name] at [Email] or [Phone Number].
Best,
[Your Name]
[Your Role/School Name]

Student Script: Introducing the ORF Program to Students
Welcome Students
(Gather students together in a welcoming, encouraging tone)
Hey [Student’s Name]! I’m excited to share that you are going to be part of a reading practice
group that will help you become a stronger, faster, and more confident reader!

What is this group about?
We are going to spend three short sessions each week practicing reading. Each session will be
about 15-20 minutes and will help you read more smoothly, with expression, and at a good pace.

What will we do?
● First, we’ll check where you’re starting from with a cold read by reading a short passage
together. You’ll get a personal reading goal just for you!
● Next, we’ll practice together—sometimes reading with a partner, sometimes echo reading,
and sometimes reading on our own.
● Finally, you’ll do a "warm read" to see how much you’ve improved and track your progress
on a special graph.

Why are we doing this?
Fluent readers don’t just read faster—they also understand what they read better! The more we
practice, the easier reading will become.

What’s the goal?
Your goal is to get better each week by practicing and improving step by step. When you meet
your goal, we’ll celebrate your progress!
