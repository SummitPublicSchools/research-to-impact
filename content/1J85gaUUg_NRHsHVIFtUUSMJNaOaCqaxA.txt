Bringing ELL Teachers into Co-Planning
How STEM Prep Academy Designed More Inclusive Co-Planning Teams
Early in 2025–26, STEM Prep's English learner (EL) teachers joined the EE/ELA co-planning
meetings at every grade level. Planning with four teachers from two specialist areas was hard at
first. After a January reset, teams found a routine that lets them plan for students learning
English and students with exceptionalities together.
[PHOTO 1: Four-person ELA and EL co-planning team. Caption: TBD]

The Challenge
In 2024–25, co-planning at STEM Prep happened in pairs: an exceptional education (EE)
teacher and an ELA teacher. ELL teachers were not part of the IGNITE co-planning structure.
Many students in co-taught ELA classes were learning English, and some were dually identified.
Yet planning for language access happened outside the EE/ELA co-planning meeting.
The idea: if all the teachers were in the room together, they could plan for the needs of both
major subgroups at the same time. That would create continuity and alignment across the ELL
teacher, the special education teacher, and the ELA teacher.
STEM Prep wanted teachers to plan for the needs of all their students, given the school's
diverse learner profiles. Rather than holding multiple separate meetings that could cause
misalignment, bringing all the teachers together would create alignment across everyone
supporting the students they share.

What They Did
Early in the 2025–26 school year, EL teachers began joining the co-planning meetings at each
grade level. At first it didn't work well. With four teachers from two specialist areas in the room,
teams weren't getting through enough of the agenda and weren't sure how to co-plan as a
group of four.
In January 2026, the school leadership planned a co-planning reset. Leaders re-normed teams
on the co-planning vision and agenda, then reimagined the routine to fit a four-person model,
co-facilitating meetings until teams could run them on their own. All ELA and EL teachers now
share a common planning period.

How it Works
● Plan together. The ELL teacher joins co-planning meetings with the EE and ELA
teachers, so all of the teachers plan instruction together.
● Decide delivery together. The team plans how they will deliver instruction, together or
separately.
● Push in to co-teach. The ELL teacher pushes into the classroom at times to co-teach or
support students alongside the special education and general education teachers.
● Support one another. Each teacher supports the others within the model.

What the Shared Planning Time Covers
All four teachers review the upcoming goals and priorities, the barriers and needs for students,
and how to differentiate based on student learning profiles. That includes special education
supports as well as multilingual learner supports and strategies.
The ELL teacher co-teaches regularly alongside all of the teachers.

Results
In 6th grade ELA, the share of students on grade level rose from 30% in the first quarter to 48%
overall, beating the school's 28% achievement target. For students with exceptionalities, it rose
from 14% to 24%.
Source: STEM Prep school data, 2025–26, as reported by the principal.
Teacher-reported impact from STEM Prep's end-of-year reflections (4 co-teaching pairs):
● improved alignment between general and special education instruction: three of four
co-teaching pairs named it a top impact
● all four pairs reported co-planning quality improved over the year

In the year-end educator survey, six of seven STEM Prep co-planning teachers said co-planning
was valuable for students with disabilities, and six of seven plan to continue. Four of seven said
it improved alignment with their partner.
STEM Prep plans to scale co-planning to middle school math, ELL and dually identified
co-taught classes, and high school EE/ELL co-teachers.

Lessons Learned
● Customize co-planning to meet the needs of your learners. If your school has other
priority populations, make the practice work for them. Including other specialists in a
co-planning model is possible.
● Give the adaptation time. It may take time to devise a way of working that makes the
practice fit a new model. STEM Prep's first approach didn't work, and the January reset
is what made it click.
● Planning together builds alignment and capacity. Bringing teams together to plan for
multiple specialist needs builds alignment and capacity across a set of specialists, and
supports a campus's content and differentiation priorities.
● Protect the time. A shared planning period for all ELA and EL teachers made
four-person planning possible.
● Start with one team and let it spread. Once teachers saw results, other co-teaching
pairs asked to join, and teachers began requesting co-planning in other content areas.

Try it at Your School
● Name your school's priority student groups, then personalize the co-planning agenda
and routine to plan for them.
● Get creative about ways educators can collaborate and co-plan when time is tight or a
campus has multiple academic or specialist needs.
● Check the master schedule: can every specialist who serves a shared group of students
plan at the same time?
● Open each meeting with data for each student group so the whole team sees where
gaps are.
